## Native LeLab resume compatibility

LeLab's native `config_path` request points to the source checkpoint's `pretrained_model/train_config.json`; `resume=true`, `steps=15000`, `save_freq=5000`, and `optimizer_type=adamw` are explicit. Specifying AdamW matters: LeLab defaults to Adam, and LeRobot does not reapply its optimizer preset on resume.

The repository adapter now joins the `--config_path` flag and its value with `=`. The pinned LeRobot resume resolver reads `sys.argv` directly and does not recognize the split form emitted by LeLab. This is a project adapter fix; installed packages are unchanged. A dry run through LeRobot's actual parser and `validate()` verified the source checkpoint, model path, optimizer, device, and requested settings before submission. The idle LeLab server was reloaded to apply the fix.

The live log confirms local weight loading and resumed sampling at epoch 3, sample 332, matching step 2,000 with this 556-frame episode. LeLab's progress bar counts the **13,000 remaining updates**, while saved checkpoint names use total steps. Thus 3,000 / 13,000 in this job corresponds to the first new checkpoint at total step 5,000.

## Loss history and convergence

The full curve combines all 300 logged averages from total step 50 through 15,000. Each point averages 50 training updates; the plotted trend is a trailing 500-update mean. Exact step numbers come from the progress counter, with a 2,000-step offset for the resumed run, because the human-readable `step:15K` fields are rounded. All resumed points match the earlier summary exactly.

The lowest **logged** loss is **0.285**, tied at total steps **14,350 and 14,800** to the log's three-decimal precision. Final loss is 0.288. These intermediate minima were not saved as checkpoints; saving remained every 5,000 steps.

There is no clear convergence plateau by 15,000 updates. Improvement becomes smaller in absolute terms, but the mean loss over updates 14,001–15,000 is **0.3034**, compared with **0.3316** over 13,001–14,000: another **8.5% decrease**. The end of this bounded run establishes its lowest observed training loss, not the attainable minimum or best validation performance. No validation loss was recorded.
